The Whispering Iroko

Zainab Yusuf (SS3) Published: June 2026

In the village of Omu, the grandfather of all trees was a giant Iroko that stood at the edge of the forest. The elders said it had witnessed the rise and fall of kingdoms, and that it spoke only to those who knew how to listen. Most of the village children feared the dark, rustling canopy, but Ngozi was different.

Every afternoon after school, Ngozi would sit beneath its massive roots, her homework spread on her knees. One windy day, as she struggled with a difficult science equation, she heard a soft, melodic sigh. It did not sound like wind. It sounded like an old man humming.

Ngozi looked up. The leaves of the Iroko were shimmering, even though the surrounding bushes were still. "Have courage, little one," a deep voice seemed to reverberate through the ground beneath her. Ngozi gasped. Over the coming weeks, she discovered that the tree did not speak in human words, but in vibrations and soft images that flooded her mind. It told her stories of ancient harvests, of brave warriors, and of a forgotten spring deep in the hills that could save the village during the coming dry season.

When the drought finally arrived, drying up the village streams, Ngozi stood before the village council. Following the whispered directions of the giant Iroko, she led the search team to the hidden mountain spring. As clean water rushed into their buckets, the elders realized that the young girl had indeed heard the wisdom of the ancient forest.

The Lost Pencil Case

Tunde Alao (JS1) Published: April 2026

It was a rainy Tuesday morning when Tunde realized his favorite blue pencil case was missing. It wasn't just any pencil case—it was a gift from his uncle, equipped with a mechanical compass and a secret compartment.

His search began in the JSS1 classroom, under desks and behind the blackboard, but there was no sign of it. Just as he was about to give up, he found a small piece of folded paper inside his desk. It read: "If you seek what is blue, follow the shadow of the tall clock at noon."

Intrigued, Tunde waited until lunch. When the hands of the administrative block clock met at twelve, its long shadow pointed directly toward the entrance of the school library. Inside the library, tucked between pages of an old geography atlas, he found another clue: "Look where the green vines climb the library wall."

Outside by the vine-covered trellis, Tunde met Amina, a classmate who was also holding a folded note. Together, they followed the final clue to the science lab cupboards. There, sitting neatly on the shelf, was Tunde's blue pencil case. Beside it was a note from their science teacher, Mr. Okon: "Congratulations on solving the riddle. Teamwork and curiosity are the best tools for any scientist." Tunde got his pencil case back, and gained a new best friend in the process.
